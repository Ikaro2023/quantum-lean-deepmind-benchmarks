# Track 3, missions 3 and 4: degree certificates

On the 23 raw magnitude bits after endpoint relaying, the 440 certified dyadic cubes partition all 8,388,608 words. Fix all stashed and external data to constants and leave these 23 bits arbitrary. The frozen table `trS2a` has degree 18 on wires 50 and 55. The frozen table `trS6cIn` has degree 18 on wires 72 and 81. The coefficient of the monomial on bits 5 through 22 is one in each case. Run `python -B degree.py` to regenerate the exhaustive GF(2) subset Möbius transform and the per-wire `degree_receipt.json`.

## Degree bound for the table interface

One quadratic assignment between affine operations increases the possible final degree by at most one, counted by backward substitution. Write polynomials multilinearly over Boolean inputs. Replacing the symbol for the newly assigned wire by a quadratic in older wires increases the degree of any monomial by at most one. An affine substitution cannot increase its degree. Repeating the substitutions bounds the degree of any output by one plus the number of quadratic assignments.

In the forward direction, an `unquad` or `unand` sets its target to zero and therefore contributes an affine assignment. The X, CX, Z and CZ operations act affinely on basis-state bits. Both `rccx` variants have quadratic bit updates and cost four T. The clean-target AND also costs four T. A statically valid `hGadget` writes a quadratic Boolean function into a clean target. Indeed its bit-one accumulator is the second-lowest bit of a signed sum of affine Boolean parities, given by a sum of pairwise products and sign-dependent linear terms. The two lists have equal lengths; with only one positive and one negative step the static zero-low-bit condition makes their parities equal, giving a zero sum. A nonlinear write therefore costs at least four T. Thus s2a requires at least 17 nonlinear writes and 68 T, attained by the original table.

For the cleanup direction, run the valid deterministic bit trace backward on this fixed domain. The reverse of `unand` or `unquad` introduces a quadratic word into a clean wire. If that word lies in the old affine span of all wires, use an affine assignment in the degree argument. Otherwise this step contributes one quadratic assignment and raises affine-span dimension by one. A reversed clean paid write erases its value; the span dimension can decrease by at most one. A reversed `rccx` replaces one wire by itself XOR a product of two other wires. If span dimension drops by one, its new word is affine in the other wires and costs zero quadratic assignments in the degree argument. Otherwise count at most one quadratic assignment for that step. Phases do not affect this necessary bit-trace condition. Linear creations or erasures arising from zero- or two-T hGadgets have zero rank cost because the generated word is identically zero.

Let p count paid operations of cost at least four T. For each step with affine-rank change d, its contribution to the number of required quadratic substitutions is at most d plus its indicator of membership among these p operations. The sum of rank changes is at most 12: s6c adds just 12 garbage coordinates to the same 23 raw bits in reverse. Therefore its starting garbage functions have degree at most 1 + 12 + p. Degree 18 implies p at least 5, so the frozen s6c interface requires at least 20 T. The original table attains the bound.

These are mathematical bounds with a machine-checked finite degree calculation on the exact frozen bit tables. No formal Lean proof of the lower-bound argument or general lower bound for different stage interfaces or arbitrary quantum circuits is asserted.
